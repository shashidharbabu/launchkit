# LinkedIn launch post rulebook (`linkedin_post`)

Reference for the `linkedin_post` platform rulebook in Launch Kit. Written 2026-09-11 from the USE and PARTIAL verdicts in `docs/social-launch-skills-review.md`, judged against `.claude/rules/skills/brand-check/references/rulebook.md` sections 1 to 3. Reconciled with the code on 2026-09-12 for **rulebook version 4** (`RULEBOOK_VERSION = 4` in `src/lib/rulebooks.ts`), against the ten-app evaluation in `docs/EVAL-10-APPS.md` and the per-draft judgements in `docs/eval-10/judged.json`.

## Scope

Launch Kit drafts one LinkedIn launch post for the builder's personal account, plus one alternate hook. The output shape is fixed by `pipelines/lk_assets.pipe` line 33:

```
{"post": string, "alt_hook": string, "warnings": [string]}
```

This document holds six things:

1. The rules the drafting model receives verbatim as the `PLATFORM_RULES` block (`src/data/rules.ts` builds it; `src/lib/rulebooks.ts` holds the default). 24 rules at version 4.
2. The mechanical checks a program can run on `post` and `alt_hook` after the sanitiser passes. 33 checks at version 4, 11 of them hard.
3. Hook patterns in our voice that seed line 1 and `alt_hook`. 10 patterns at version 4.
4. A short launch-week rule set (cadence, reply triage and the 48-hour metric) the model carries into `warnings`, never into the post body.
5. How a stored rulebook goes stale, what every draft records about the rules it saw, and which sanitiser passes run before the gate.
6. The questions the owner still has to answer, with their evidence.

Nothing here posts anything. The builder pastes the post. That is the source pack's design and it is also the brand rulebook's approval workflow (section 5.5).

## What the humanizer strips and scores (li-human)

Read in full because the checks below are modelled on it.

`humanize.py` runs three passes and reports a fourth:

1. Invisible characters: deletes zero-width space and joiners, word joiner, BOM, soft hyphen, Mongolian vowel separator, Arabic letter mark, LTR and RTL marks, Unicode tag characters (U+E0000 to U+E007F), and any leftover Unicode `Cf` character; turns no-break, narrow no-break, thin, figure, em and en spaces into plain spaces.
2. Typography: em dash to `, `, en dash between digits to `-` and elsewhere to `, `, curly quotes to straight, ellipsis to three dots, bullet and middle dot to `-`, arrow to `->`. Then it cleans the doubled punctuation an em dash leaves behind.
3. Lexicon: 113 stock words and phrases from `slop.json` (verbs, adjectives, nouns, connectives, openers, closers), replaced with plain words, longest match first, capitalisation preserved, URLs protected.
4. Structural tells, flagged only: "It's not just X, it's Y", "not only X but also Y", "It isn't about X. It's about Y", one-word question lines ("The result?"), "Here's what I learned", comma triads, the five reflex emoji (rocket, fire, bulb, sparkle, dart), hashtag walls of five or more, model self-reference, essay closers ("In summary"), reflex engagement bait ("Thoughts?", "Agree?", "Who else"), and uniform sentence length (coefficient of variation under 0.35 across four or more sentences).

`detect.py` scores five signals 0 to 100 and takes 60 percent of the mean plus 40 percent of the weakest check. PASS needs 70 overall with no check under 55:

| Check | Measures | Human target it uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Burstiness | sentence-length variation | coefficient of variation 0.55 or more |
| Specificity | numbers plus distinct proper nouns per 100 words | 4 or more |
| Slop density | lexicon hits per 100 words | 0 |
| Fingerprint | invisible chars (weight 4), em dashes (2), curly quotes, ellipses, hard spaces per 1,000 chars | 0 |
| Voice | contractions per 100 words (target 3), personal pronouns per 100 words (target 8), structural tells, uniform bullet length | as stated |

What was carried into this rulebook: the dash pass (`sanitizeDraft`), the invisible-character pass (the `invisible_chars` check), the lexicon (split in v4 between `SLOP_SWAPS`, which `cleanSlop` applies before the gate, and the `slop_lexicon` warning check), the structural tells (two checks and two rules), and the burstiness, specificity and voice targets (rule 9, since they need a scorer rather than a regex). The scripts themselves are not vendored; see notes.

## Rules adopted

The live rules in the order `src/lib/rulebooks.ts` lists them, which is the order the model receives them. Each rule is followed by where it came from and what changed. A rule marked **New in v4** was not in version 3; a rule marked **Changed in v4** is quoted here in its new wording.

1. **Line 1 is the hook and stands alone at 140 characters or fewer: one specific, true thing about this app (a number from proof_points, a named failure, a concrete before and after). No question mark in line 1 unless only this builder could ask the question. No emoji in line 1.**
   From li-post ("It has to survive truncation at ~140 chars mobile") and `hooks.json` rules ("No question marks in line 1 unless the question is specific enough that only you could ask it"). Changed: tied "specific" to the app profile, added the no-emoji clause from the slop.json rocket rule. New in v4: the wording stands, the enforcement moved. `hook_line_max_chars` now carries `hard: true`, because it fired on the cal-com draft (line 1 at 147 characters) and the draft was approved anyway (docs/EVAL-10-APPS.md finding 1).

2. **Line 2 pays off line 1. It is never setup for line 3. If a reader stops after two lines they must still have the point.**
   From `hooks.json` rule 2 and li-post's shape ("the payoff of line 1, not setup for line 3"). Changed: added the stop-after-two-lines test.

3. **Length 120 to 200 words (about 900 to 1,300 characters). Under 120 reads as a passing thought; over 200 has to earn every line and the see-more tap has to be paid for by line 2.**
   From li-post ("900-1,300 characters is the working range"; "Under 400 reads as a thought") reconciled with the existing Launch Kit default and the pipe (120 to 200 words). Changed: kept the word range the pipe already states; added the character equivalent so both agree. The range is written with the word "to" on purpose: `sanitize.ts` `EN_RANGE` turns an unspaced en dash inside a range into a hyphen and any other one into a comma, which mangled price ranges in the eval (docs/EVAL-10-APPS.md finding 9).

4. *Changed in v4.* **One idea per post, in this order: the one true thing the profile holds (a proof point, a named constraint, an incident from why_built), the app in one plain sentence (its name once, what it does once), what the reader can do with it today. When the profile holds no incident, open on the proof point or the function; never write the bug, the first user or the moment you decided to build it from imagination. If the draft holds two ideas, keep the stronger one and list the other in warnings as a second post.**
   From li-post SKILL.md line 79 ("One idea per post. If the draft has two, you have two posts. Say so") and RocketRide, this task (docs/eval-10/judged.json hack-judge and hoppscotch; `GLOBAL_RULES[9]`). Changed in v4: the first slot used to ask for "what happened while building (the bug, the constraint, the first user)" unconditionally, and on ten of ten eval apps the model supplied a scene the profile never held (docs/EVAL-10-APPS.md finding 3 names the rulebook wording as the cause). The slot now asks for the one true thing the profile holds and says what to open on when there is no incident.

5. **Every number, name, customer and outcome comes from APP_PROFILE.proof_points or BRAND_DNA. If the post needs a number you do not have, write {{your number}} in its place and add a warning. Never invent users, metrics, testimonials, benchmarks or partnerships, and never write a revenue or funding figure.**
   From li-post ("Numbers over adjectives"; "Never fabricate... leave {{your number}} in the draft and flag it") and brand rulebook 2.7 and 5.1 (financial projections confidential; do not assert capability without confirmation). Changed: named the two Launch Kit sources of truth; added partnerships and money to the never-invent list per rulebook 4.4 and 5.1.

6. **No URL and no {APP_URL} in the post body; LinkedIn suppresses posts with outbound links. End the body with the line "Link in the first comment." and add the warning "First comment: {APP_URL}" so the builder pastes it there.**
   From li-post ("No links in the post body... Put the link in the first comment and say so in the receipt"). Changed: mapped "the receipt" onto the `warnings` array, which is the only other field in the output shape. This changes where {APP_URL} lands for LinkedIn only.

7. **Close with exactly one of: a question only this post could ask, or one instruction. Never both. Never "Thoughts?", "Agree?", "Who else?", "Am I wrong?", "Comment YES", "Tag someone", "Repost if", "Follow me for more", "Save this", "Let that sink in", "Read that again".**
   From li-post ("one specific question, or one instruction. Never both"; "No engagement bait"), slop.json closers and the `engagement-bait` structure, cross-checked with the hoai anti-pattern list ("Comment YES if", "Share this with 3 people", "Follow me for more"). Changed: merged the three lists into one, dropped nothing. New in v4: the ask side of this rule is now a hard check (`li_no_vote_ask`), ported byte-identical from the x_post list.

8. **Paragraphs of one to three lines with a blank line between every one. No wall of text, no run of six or more one-sentence lines, at most three bullet lines, no line in ALL CAPS, no Title Case headline.**
   From li-post's shape ("short paragraphs, 1-3 lines each, blank line between every one... The white space is the format"), the existing Launch Kit rule ("No bullet-list dumps"), and hoai format anti-patterns ("Every. Sentence. As. Its. Own. Line.", "ALL CAPS FOR EMPHASIS"). Changed: gave the anti-patterns numbers so they can be checked.

9. **Write in first person with contractions (it's, we've, didn't): aim for three contractions and eight personal pronouns per 100 words. Vary sentence length: at least one sentence under six words and one over twenty, never four sentences in a row of the same length.**
   From `detect.py` VOICE (contractions target 3.0 per 100, pronouns 8.0 per 100) and BURSTINESS (variation 0.55 or more; "Break one sentence in half. Let another run long. Machines write even."). Changed: turned the scorer's thresholds into instructions the model can follow without running the script.

10. **Never these shapes: "It's not X, it's Y"; "not only X but also Y"; "This isn't about X. It's about Y"; a one-word question line such as "The result?"; "Here's what I learned"; a closing paragraph that opens "In summary" or "In conclusion"; a comma triad of three adjectives.**
    From slop.json `structures` (all eleven read; nine kept here, the hashtag wall and emoji moved to their own rules, model self-reference now carried by a check rather than the global filler rule). Unchanged in v4, and this is the one place where the rule text is narrower than its check: the rule names three contrast forms, while `contrast_shape` (hard, new in v4) fails eight. The v4 audit proposed widening the rule's wording to match; the merge did not apply it. See "Open questions".

11. **For releasing software say launch, release, deploy, deliver or roll out; never the verb spelled s-h-i-p in any form. Never the deprecated one-key-for-every-model framing (section 1.2). Never the slop lexicon: delve, leverage, utilize, robust, crucial, vital, comprehensive, journey, landscape, realm, paradigm, synergy, testament to, cornerstone, empower, streamline, foster, facilitate, showcase, moreover, furthermore, additionally, ultimately, "in order to", "when it comes to", "in today's fast-paced world", "here's the thing", "let's face it", "excited to announce", "thrilled to share". No hype adjectives and no speed multipliers ("N times faster").**
    From slop.json `words` and `phrases`, brand rulebook 1.1 (banned words) and 1.3 (AI tells), and the task brief's hype set. Changed: two lexicon entries were dropped because they are RocketRide's own vocabulary (see "left out"); the banned launch verb and the deprecated key framing were added; the eleven filler terms already listed in `GLOBAL_RULES` are not repeated in the rule but stay in the check. New in v4: the verb is written out letter by letter, as `GLOBAL_RULES[7]` writes it, and 67 of the lexicon terms no longer reach the gate at all because `cleanSlop` swaps them first (see "Storage and versioning").

12. *Changed in v4.* **Never write "better than", "unlike other tools", "outperforms" or "alternative to", and never attach a negative word to another product's name. Another product may appear only as a neutral fact the profile states (what it is, a public price, that a named customer moved from it). A testimonial that compares to another product is dropped or pasted whole into the first comment through warnings; it is never rewritten. Show the thing working instead: one concrete detail of what it did, on what input. Name a partner only if it is in proof_points.**
    From brand rulebook 4.1 ("Never position RocketRide as better than X. Show, don't tell"), 4.2 (factual comparisons are on-brand), 4.4 (partners in discussion must not read as confirmed), 3.1 ("Show, don't tell"), `GLOBAL_RULES[10]`, and RocketRide, this task (docs/eval-10/judged.json dub, plausible, cal-com). Changed in v4: the rule used to open "Never name a competitor", which was stricter than `GLOBAL_RULES[10]` and brand 4.2, and PLATFORM_RULES override the global rules in the pipe. The collision made the model strip a competitor's name out of a verbatim customer quote (dub) and out of a relative number (plausible: "135KB less than that"). The new wording keeps every brand ban, allows the neutral factual mention the brand rulebook allows, and says what to do with a comparing testimonial. Flagged for Joe: see "Open questions".

13. **Hashtags: at most three, all on the last line after the link line, only real categories people follow (for example #opensource #devtools #python). None inline in the body.**
    From li-post ("Three hashtags maximum, at the bottom, and only if they are real categories someone follows") and hoai ("Never use hashtags inline with text"). Changed: hoai allows five; li-post and the existing Launch Kit rule say three; three wins.

14. **Emoji: at most one in the whole post, never in line 1, and never rocket, fire, light bulb, sparkles or dart. Normal capitalisation and full punctuation throughout: this is a post, not a DM.**
    From slop.json `rocket` structure ("One emoji max, and not one of these five"), `GLOBAL_RULES` (at most one) and brand rulebook 3.2 (LinkedIn posts use normal capitalisation; lowercase openers are for Joe's DMs only). Changed: none. New in v4: `banned_emoji` reads `alt_hook` too, because an alternate hook is a line 1.

15. *Changed in v4.* **alt_hook is a second line 1 for the same post from a different hook family than the one used (see the hook patterns): 140 characters or fewer, one line, standing alone, no URL, no question mark unless the question is specific to this builder. If line 1 opens with a number, alt_hook opens with something other than a number; a second count from the same profile is the same family, not a different one.**
    From li-post's loop ("Pick three hooks, not one... Different formulas, not three variations of one", SKILL.md lines 49 to 52) and RocketRide, this task (docs/eval-10/judged.json continue: alt_hook "5,359 developers forked Continue" beside line 1 "35,870 developers starred Continue"). Changed in v4: added the number test. The model called a second count from the same profile "a different hook family"; a cross-field check would need a new check kind, so the number test is the proxy the model can apply itself.

16. **warnings must list: the first-comment link line, every {{your number}} placeholder, any second idea cut from the draft, and any claim you could not trace to proof_points.**
    From li-post's receipt block and its `{{your number}}` flag, plus brand rulebook 5.3 (unsupported capability claims get flagged). Changed: mapped onto the `warnings` field.

17. *Changed in v4.* **Launch week, for warnings not the post, as one warnings line of at most 60 words that names this app's four subjects, never a generic reminder: after the launch post, at most four posts in the week and never two of the same kind back to back: proof (the one number from this launch), opinion (a position on the problem this app solves), teach (one thing the reader can do with this app today), story (a scene from building it with what it cost, only if the profile holds one). Tuesday to Thursday, 7:30 to 9:30 am in the audience's timezone; the first line matters far more than the hour.**
    From li-plan SKILL.md lines 29 to 56 ("Four posts a week beats seven"; the proof / opinion / teach / story / offer table; "Tuesday to Thursday, 7:30-9:30am local"; "the day and hour matter far less than whether the first line is good") and RocketRide, this task (docs/eval-10/judged.json documenso: "the same boilerplate for every app"). Changed: the "offer" slot is dropped because the launch post is the offer. Changed in v4: capped at one warnings line of at most 60 words that names this app's four subjects, because all ten eval drafts carried a near-identical 80-word cadence paragraph; the story slot is now conditional on the profile holding a scene.

18. *Changed in v4.* **Launch day replies, for warnings not the post, as one warnings line of at most 60 words: in the first hour sort every comment into lead, substance, peer, support, noise and answer in that order; answer the question fully in public, use the commenter's name once with no exclamation mark, match their length; to a critic concede the true part first in their words, then hold the line; never delete, never reply twice; ignore pitches; never ask for likes, reposts, tags or comments as a favour.**
    From li-reply SKILL.md lines 23 to 49 (the five buckets and every "How to reply" bullet) and li-comment SKILL.md lines 40 to 53, with the no-vote-ask clause from the task brief and the hoai "What Not to Do" list. Changed: condensed to one rule; the DM open-door line was dropped (outreach is out of scope). Changed in v4: capped at one warnings line of at most 60 words. Substance unchanged.

19. *New in v4.* **A sentence inside quote marks is verbatim from APP_PROFILE.proof_points or BRAND_DNA and is attributed exactly as the source attributes it: the name and role given, no pronoun the source does not give. If the quote cannot run whole (a competitor comparison inside it, length), keep the fact without quote marks or drop it and add the warning "quote dropped: <its first words>"; never rewrite a quote and present it as said.**
    From RocketRide, this task (docs/eval-10/judged.json dub: "Dub is hands down the best link platform I've used", printed in quote marks after the real quote's competitor name was removed; cal-com: the Flo Merian line rewritten and given a pronoun the source does not give) and brand rulebook 1.3 and 2.7. Rule 5 already said never invent a testimonial; nothing said what a quote mark promises.

20. *New in v4.* **A star, fork, download or click count is a count and nothing more. Never write why the people behind it starred, forked, downloaded or clicked ("most of them came from", "most of those people wanted") unless proof_points states the reason; write the number, then what the app does.**
    From RocketRide, this task (docs/eval-10/judged.json formbricks line 1, khoj line 1, dub hook). Three of ten LinkedIn hooks turned a star or click count into a claim about the motives of thousands of strangers, which no profile holds. The receipt hook invites the number; this rule says where the number stops.

21. *New in v4.* **A number that only means something against another product (N KB smaller than X, N times faster than Y) loses its meaning when the name goes. Drop the number rather than write "less than that" or "than anything else", and add the warning "comparison dropped: <the number and what it was measured against>".**
    From RocketRide, this task (docs/eval-10/judged.json plausible: "So we built Plausible to load 135KB less than that"; cal-com: "than anything else she'd tried") and brand rulebook 4.1. Rule 12 removed the competitor's name and the model kept the relative number, so the hook argued against nothing. `dangling_comparative` flags the residue; this rule says what to do instead.

22. *New in v4.* **Carry the profile's own qualifier on every claim about data, privacy or where the app runs ("unless you point it at a cloud model", "on the hosted plan", "for self-hosted installs"); never flatten a qualified claim into an absolute.**
    From RocketRide, this task (docs/eval-10/judged.json khoj: "Either way, your documents don't leave your machine" beside the profile's own cloud-model option). Four khoj drafts stated an absolute the profile itself qualifies; the Show HN body and the listing FAQ carried the qualifier and were right. Not a mechanical shape, so it stays a rule.

23. *New in v4.* **Pick a hook pattern whose every slot the profile fills. Time anchor, cost of a wrong assumption, the log line, the practice I stopped and the thing I deleted need a story in APP_PROFILE (why_built, a proof point with a scene, BRAND_DNA evidence); when there is none, open with the receipt, the plain function or the state of the project, and never invent the scene, the practice or the assumption.**
    From RocketRide, this task (docs/eval-10/judged.json hack-judge, where the judge traced four invented anecdotes to the hook list; hoppscotch "I built it because API development tooling kept drifting"; khoj's alt_hook practice that nobody holds) and `GLOBAL_RULES[9]`. Five of the eight original hook patterns carry a story slot. The audit's schema has no change_hooks field, so the guard is a rule that governs the hook list, plus the two profile-only hooks added below.

24. *New in v4.* **Add one warnings line after the reply line: "48 hours after posting, record engagement rate (reactions plus comments plus reposts, divided by impressions) and reach multiple (impressions divided by followers), not impressions; that is the number the next post is judged on."**
    From `linkedin-agent-skill/skills/li-audit/SKILL.md`, the "What to actually measure" table and "Rank by engagement rate and reach multiple, not impressions. A post with 900 impressions and 40 comments beat the one with 12,000 impressions and 6" (rewritten; MIT). li-audit is the one LinkedIn PARTIAL verdict (review line 98) that the first version of this document never mentioned. Only the metric line is taken; the rest of li-audit stays deferred (see "left out").

`GLOBAL_RULES` (12 entries) is appended to every platform's block by `rulesBlock`, so the drafting model sees it as well: the dash ban, the AI-filler ban, the no-rhetorical-opener rule, sentence case, one concrete detail over three adjectives, the builder's own voice, at most one emoji, the banned launch verb written letter by letter, and the four rules added for v4 (never invent a person, never invent an origin story or a limitation, a competitor only as a neutral fact, and the thin-profile rule). Where a platform rule and a global rule collide, the platform rule wins in the pipe; that collision is what rule 12 had to fix.

## Mechanical checks

The gate compiles each `forbidden_regex` with `flags` when the check sets it, otherwise `"i"`, and `"iu"` when the value carries a `\u{...}` escape. `post_all_caps_run` sets `flags: ""` so it runs case-sensitive. The multiline flag is never applied: the patterns use `(?:^|\n)` where a line start is meant, and `^` alone means start of string. Values below are written the way a JavaScript engine reads them (single backslashes), not the way the TypeScript literal spells them. The two dash characters are written as `\u2014` and `\u2013`, and the invisible characters as `\uXXXX` escapes, so this file itself contains none of them.

Three runner behaviours matter to how these read, all new since the first version of this document:

- **`hard`.** A check may set `hard` directly; otherwise the gate derives it from the kind (`max_chars`, `max_words`, `max_count`, `required_prefix`, `required_regex`) or from an id pattern (`brand_banned`, `no_dashes`, `url_in_body`, `vote_ask`, `link_present` and the rest). A hard hit is written to `data.blockers` as well as `data.warnings`, it blocks approval, and it triggers the one repair pass in `src/data/api.ts` `runAsset`. 11 of the 33 checks below are hard.
- **`field`.** A named field, or `all` (equivalently `*`) for every string field and every string inside an array field, except the stamped metadata: `fieldsOf` skips `META_FIELDS`, which is `warnings`, `blockers`, `repaired`, `venue`, `app_name`, `rulebook_version`, `rulebook_source`, `punctuation_fixed`, `wording_fixed` and `slop_fixed`. On this platform `all` therefore means `post` and `alt_hook`.
- **`when`.** An optional guard `{ field, regex }` that runs a check only when another field matches. It exists for the venue-scoped Reddit title checks, which read the `venue` field the runner stamps. No `linkedin_post` check uses it.

Two kinds the first version of this document did not have: `required_regex` (the first entry of the field must match, and a pattern JavaScript cannot compile is treated as a pass) and `max_count` (counted by `countIn`, which counts `{APP_URL}` placeholders for an id containing `url`, raw links for `raw_links`, paragraph breaks for `paragraph`, occurrences of the product's name from the `app_name` stamp for `product_name`, and otherwise hashtags). No `linkedin_post` check uses either kind; both are noted because the document's checks table now shares a runner with platforms that do.

## Hook patterns

Ten patterns for line 1 and `alt_hook`, in the order `src/lib/rulebooks.ts` lists them. Each of the first eight keeps the mechanics of one `hooks.json` formula (the template shape and its trap) and drops the source example, which is written for agency owners and cold-DM senders. The last two are ours, added in v4 for the profile that holds no story. Slots in braces are filled from the app profile; every number must come from proof_points, and rule 23 now forbids picking a pattern whose slots the profile cannot fill.

1. Time anchor: "{Task} used to take me {long time}. It now takes {short time}. One {file, component or decision} did it." Both times must come from proof_points; a ratio the reader cannot believe kills the post.
   Mechanics of `hooks.json` formula 17.

2. Cost of a wrong assumption: "{N hours or days} is what {one wrong assumption} cost me before I built {app}." Name the assumption, make the cost real, and give the fix by line 4.
   Formula 3. Trap: a fake-humble mistake.

3. The log line: "\"{The exact line from the stack trace, the PR review or the first user's message.}\" That line is why {app} exists." Use the real text, including the ugly part.
   Formula 9. Trap: dialogue nobody said.

4. The receipt: "{One hard number from proof_points}. {Where it ran, on what input, with what model.}" The number opens the door; the second sentence is the room.
   Formula 10. Trap: a number with no story.

5. The practice I stopped: "Everyone building with {LLMs, agents, RAG} says {common practice}. After {specific experience with app}, I stopped." The practice has to be genuinely common, and you say what replaced it.
   Formula 1. Trap: being contrarian about something nobody believes.

6. The thing I deleted: "I deleted {thing developers usually keep: the vector DB, the retry wrapper, the second model} from {app}. Here is what broke and what got faster." Name the cost, not only the win.
   Formulas 18 and 20. Trap: a walk-away with no cost.

7. Two ways, one verdict: "A {heavy option: five-service pipeline, hosted platform} vs {the small thing I built in a weekend}. Here is where each one wins." Concede what the heavy option does better; never name a competitor.
   Formula 12. Trap: an unfair comparison.

8. Copy this: "Here is the exact {config, prompt, .pipe file, script} I use to {specific outcome}. Copy it." Give the artifact in the post, not behind a comment-for-link.
   Formula 21. Trap: making the reader ask for the artifact.

9. The plain function: "{App} takes {input} and gives you {output}. {One checkable fact from the profile: the license, where it runs, what it plugs into}." Use when the profile holds no story; every slot comes from APP_PROFILE and nothing is invented. Trap: an adjective in the output slot. Name the artifact, not its quality.

## Storage and versioning

- **This document matches rulebook version 4.** `RULEBOOK_VERSION = 4` in `src/lib/rulebooks.ts`. The counts it describes are 24 rules, 10 hooks and 33 checks for `linkedin_post`, plus 12 `GLOBAL_RULES`.
- **How a stored row goes stale.** `seedRulebooksIfEmpty` (`src/data/seed.ts`) writes each default into `platform_rules` with `source: 'default'` and `version: RULEBOOK_VERSION`. On a later load, the newest stored row for a platform is stale when its `source` is anything other than `owner` and its `version` is below `RULEBOOK_VERSION`; the v4 default is then inserted **above** it rather than replacing it, and `rulesFor` takes the newest row, so the store keeps a record of which version each earlier draft was written against.
- **How an owner edit survives.** `isStale` in `src/data/rules.ts` returns false for any row whose `source` is `owner`, whatever its version, so a rulebook the owner edited in Settings is never replaced by a newer default. A stored row that predates the hooks array, or an owner edit that saved none, falls back to the default hooks.
- **Every draft names its rulebook.** `rulebookMeta` returns the version and source that `rulesFor` actually used (the stored row's, or `RULEBOOK_VERSION` and `default` when the code default wins), and `src/data/api.ts` `runAsset` stamps `rulebook_version` and `rulebook_source` onto every draft. Both are in `META_FIELDS`, so no check with field `all` ever sweeps them. A re-run can therefore prove which rules a given draft saw.
- **Which sanitiser passes run before the gate, in order.** (1) `sanitizeDraft` walks every string in the model's answer inside `src/data/runner.ts` `ask`: an em dash becomes a comma, an unspaced en dash inside a range becomes a hyphen (`EN_RANGE`), any other en dash becomes a comma, and the doubled punctuation left behind is cleaned; the count is read back as `punctuation_fixed`. (2) `sanitizeVerbs` in `runAsset` runs `cleanVerbs`, which swaps the banned launch verb's four forms for the release verb, then `cleanSlop`, which applies the 67 `SLOP_SWAPS`; both preserve the capitalisation of the first letter and both skip the `warnings` field, which quotes the draft's faults as written. The counts are stamped as `wording_fixed` and `slop_fixed`. (3) The runner stamps `venue` (from the target) and `app_name`. (4) `gateAsset` runs the dash sweep over every string field, the asset-type special cases, and then every check in `RULEBOOK_CHECKS['linkedin_post']`, writing one line per hit (`field: description (evidence)`) into `warnings` and every hard hit into `blockers`. (5) If `blockers` is non-empty, `runAsset` makes one repair pass that names the cuts to make, and keeps whichever of the two drafts has the smaller overage; the kept draft records the blockers it was repaired for in `repaired`.
- **`ASSET_LIMITS` still has no `linkedin_post` entry**, and needs none: `post_max_chars` and `alt_hook_max_chars` are hard by kind, so the caps are enforced through the check list instead.

## Open questions

Unresolved. Each one needs the owner's decision, not this document's.

1. **A verbatim testimonial that names a competitor.** May a builder's LinkedIn post quote, word for word, a customer line that compares the app to a named competitor ("More elegant than Calendly")? Rule 12 as merged drops such a quote or moves it whole to the first comment through `warnings`. The alternative, quoting it verbatim in the post, is a factual third-party comparison and allowed under brand rulebook 4.2, but reads as "better than X" under 4.1. Evidence: `docs/eval-10/judged.json` dub, where the stricter version of rule 12 made the model rewrite Ian Mackey's quote (the real quote names Bitly) and print the rewrite in quote marks; cal-com, where the Flo Merian line was rewritten into "more elegant" and "more open" than anything else she'd tried, with a pronoun the source does not give. Joe's call (brand rulebook 4.1 against 4.2).
2. **Rule 10's wording against `contrast_shape`'s reach.** The rule names three contrast forms; the check fails eight and is hard, so the repair pass will act on shapes the rule never named. The v4 audit proposed the wider rule wording and the merge applied the check but not the wording. Either merge the wider wording or accept that the rule is narrower than its check. Evidence: `docs/EVAL-10-APPS.md` finding 20 and the five approved contrast lines in `docs/eval-10/judged.json` (cal-com, formbricks, hoppscotch, dub, documenso).
3. **`structural_tells` still reads only `post`.** The audit asked for field `all` on the same grounds as the other four widenings, and the merge did not apply it, so a staged-reveal tell or a model self-reference in `alt_hook` is not caught. One-word decision: widen it or say why not.
4. **`invisible_chars` holds the raw characters, not escapes.** Read back from the source, this check's value carries the characters themselves (U+200B to U+200F, U+2060, U+FEFF, U+00AD, U+202F, U+2009, U+2007, U+2003, U+2002, U+180E, U+061C), while the two dash checks carry `\u2014` and `\u2013` as escapes; the audit reports the same thing from `xxd`. The regex behaves identically today, but the same sweep that once flattened the dash class would empty this one silently. The audit asked for escapes; the merge did not apply it.
5. **Two soft checks the audit's own summary assumed were hard.** `first_comment_line_present` is soft, because the gate's id pattern is `link_present` and the id reads `line_present`; `alt_hook_no_url` is soft, because its id is not one of the gate's hard url patterns. `post_min_words` is soft too, since `min_words` is not a hard kind. If a post with no first-comment line, an alternate hook carrying a link, or a 90-word post should block approval, each needs `hard: true` set on the check.
6. **Whether a drafting rulebook should carry a post-launch metric at all.** Rule 24 is the only line in this rulebook about what happens after the post goes out. The audit offered the alternative explicitly: keep it, or record li-audit under "left out" and drop the rule. It is currently kept.
7. **The account switch.** This rulebook drafts for the builder's personal account in the builder's voice. It has no rule for a post from Joe's personal account (brand rulebook 3.5: moderate RocketRide mentions, named only when it fits the point) or from the RocketRide brand account (full marketing voice), and the review's step 2, "point voice.md at Joe's voice", was left out on purpose. If Launch Kit ever drafts RocketRide's own launch, that rule set is missing.
8. **Foundation model providers.** No check names OpenAI, Anthropic, Google or Meta, and `competitor_framing` does not route a provider name for review, although brand rulebook 4.3 asks for a flag on any comparative use and says the policy is not formally written. Whether those four names get their own routing check is a policy question and is not written here.
9. **App Builder naming.** Nothing in this rulebook names bucket 3, and no check was proposed for it. Brand rulebook 5.2 asks that any specific public name for it be flagged; if Launch Kit ever drafts for it, that check is missing.
10. **The two lexicon exclusions.** "harness" (rulebook 2.5) and "ecosystem" (rulebook 3.1) are out of the lexicon on purpose because they are RocketRide's own vocabulary, and "underscore" is out because it is a character name in developer text. If the brand rulebook changes, revisit all three.
