# Uppbeat licensing — tiers and what they actually permit

Compiled Aug 2026 from uppbeat.io/pricing, uppbeat.io/commercial-licenses,
Uppbeat's licensing blog posts, and third-party pricing reviews. Uppbeat's own
pages sit behind bot protection, so figures here came via secondary
verification of the official pages — **treat every number as "as of Aug 2026"
and re-check uppbeat.io/pricing and uppbeat.io/user-agreement before any
decision with money or a client attached.** Nothing here is legal advice.

## Tier table

| | Free | Premium (~$6.99/mo, billed yearly) | Business (top tier, ~2x Premium) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Downloads | 3/month, renewed monthly | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Catalog | ~25% of tracks + SFX | Full | Full |
| Attribution | **Required** — per-video Uppbeat Credit | None | None |
| YouTube Content ID | Cleared only by pasting the credit | Connected channels safelisted | Safelisted |
| Monetized personal channels | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Client work** | **No** | **No** | Yes |
| **Paid ads (digital/social)** | **No** | **No** | Yes (online only) |
| Org/company content | No | No | Yes |

Yearly billing discounts roughly 20% vs monthly; monthly prices are higher
than the figures above. Some third-party writeups reference a higher-priced
pro/agency tier for heavy commercial use — the tier naming has shifted over
time, which is exactly why you verify the live pricing page before relying on
a name.

## The Uppbeat Credit (free tier)

- Each free download issues a **unique, one-time credit code** tied to that
  download. It clears exactly one video.
- It must appear **in the description of the published video** (YouTube) or
  the equivalent visible metadata on other platforms. For a video embedded on
  your own site, put the credit in visible page text near the player.
- No credit → Content ID copyright claim on the video. The claim is resolvable
  by adding the credit, but on a client's or company's channel this is
  reputational damage you caused.
- Credits are per-video, not per-track: reusing last month's track in a new
  video needs a new download/credit.

## Permitted media (subscription tiers)

Per Uppbeat's license documentation: (i) videos; (ii) audio content such as
podcasts; (iii) live streaming; (iv) online games or applications. Business
extends this to content published by organizations and to **paid online
advertising** on open distribution platforms (YouTube, TikTok, Meta, Patreon,
etc.).

Web embedding follows from this: a soundtracked *video* embedded on your site
is covered content. A bare music file wired to a page as an ambient soundtrack
is a gray zone the license text doesn't bless — and it's bad practice anyway
(see SKILL.md restraint rules). If a page genuinely needs standalone audio,
ask Uppbeat support in writing first.

## Never covered, on any subscription

- **Broadcast** TV/radio, **cinema/film releases**, **live events** — Music
  Vine (Uppbeat's parent, musicvine.com) sells separate commercial licenses
  for most of the catalog for these.
- **Redistribution in any form**: sublicensing, reselling, sharing the files,
  shipping them where end users can extract and reuse them, re-hosting as
  downloadable assets, committing masters to a public repo.
- **Claiming the music as yours** / registering it with Content ID yourself.
- **AI training** on the catalog.

## Practical rules for RocketRide-style work

1. Company launch content (RocketRide marketing videos, ads) = **Business
   tier**, full stop. shashidhar.babu@rocketride.ai publishing on a company
   channel is org content even if the download happened on a personal login.
2. The license lives with the **account that downloaded**. Keep a record of
   which account/tier downloaded each track used in shipped content, and the
   download date — that's your evidence if a claim or audit happens.
3. Subscription lapse: content published while subscribed generally remains
   cleared, but re-verify the current user agreement's survival terms before
   relying on it for client deliverables.
4. When a use case isn't obviously in the permitted list, the cheap move is
   an email to Uppbeat support and a saved reply — not an interpretation.
