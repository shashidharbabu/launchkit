# Voice

Words are design material. They exist to make the product easier to understand, so they
are written from the person's side of the screen, in plain verbs, in sentence case, with
no filler.

## Register

Candid, calm, specific. The product knows what it did and says so. It does not sell, does
not cheer, does not apologise, and does not get cute.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| "Launch Kit read your site and drafted your profile." | "We've analyzed your amazing app!" |
| "Nobody is publicly asking for what your app does right now. That is common before launch." | "No results found." |
| "The backend is not reachable. Start it, then reload." | "Oops! Something went wrong." |
| "Five right venues beat fifty." | "Maximize your reach across 50+ channels." |

## Vocabulary, fixed

| Say | Never |
| --- | --- |
| launch | project, campaign (except the Brand stage's campaign ideas) |
| stage | step, phase, tab |
| gate, approve, approved | sign-off, confirm, submit, OK |
| profile, brand, commercial, assets, targets, signals, plan | any synonym |
| venue | channel, community (except as a venue kind) |
| signal | lead, mention |
| draft, regenerate | generate, AI-write |
| run | job, pipeline, task |
| tracked link, ref | UTM, attribution link |
| the backend | the server, the API, the store |

Nothing on a screen says "AI", "model", "LLM", "pipeline", or the name of any platform
the product runs on.

## Labels and buttons

- Sentence case, verb first, three words for a primary: "Start your launch", "Analyze my
  app", "Copy launch plan".
- The verb on the button is the verb in the toast: Approve produces "Approved", Copy
  produces "Copied", Save produces "Saved as v3".
- Loading labels are the progressive verb without an ellipsis: "Starting analysis",
  "Ranking venues".
- Gate approvals speak for the person: "This is right. Approve". Objections do too:
  "Something is wrong. Redo it", "Read my app again".
- Section labels are nouns in sentence case: "Short description", "Observed colors",
  "Where this came from".

## Empty states: fact, reason, next act

Title: a fact with a period ("No venues ranked yet."). Description: why, and what happens
next, honestly. Action: one verb. When the honest answer is that there is nothing, say
that ("Queue clear. Every signal is handled.").

## Errors: what happened, then the fix

One bold sentence for what happened, one plain sentence for what to do. Name the thing
("Site URL does not look like a URL: 'pingdeck'"). Never blame, never apologise, never
"unexpected".

## Provenance

Lowercase-leading fragments in mono, separated by hairlines: "drafted from repo and
site", "v2", "verified against thread", "venue rules summarized, verify before posting".
Every draft has one.

## Numbers

Always say what a number counts: "5 selected of 18 ranked", "2 with an approved
profile", "across 3 venues". No fake precision, no invented totals.

## Banned

- The em-dash and the en-dash, everywhere, including alt text and comments in copy
  files. Use a period, a comma, a colon, or two sentences.
- The middle dot as a separator.
- Exclamation marks.
- Uppercase tracked labels.
- "Seamless", "elevate", "unleash", "supercharge", "next-gen", "revolutionize".
- "Oops", "uh-oh", "whoops", "sorry".
- Version stamps, build numbers, "beta", "v0.x" in the UI.
- Referring to a person by "user"; the product says "you".
